# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Technical recruiters, engineering managers, and hiring leads evaluating Tiago Estrela Lauer for senior/lead full-stack roles (remote or on-site). They skim — seconds, not minutes — scanning for role fit and proof before deciding to read further or reach out.

## Product Purpose

A multi-page portfolio with an engineering blog (Next.js, EN/PT) that positions Tiago as a Full-Stack Technical Lead through verifiable, quantified engineering outcomes rather than adjectives. Success: a recruiter recognizes seniority and domain fit within seconds of landing on the hero, and converts to a case-study read, a blog post, or contact.

## Brand Personality

Sharp / Precise / Senior. Direct, unembellished, engineer-to-engineer voice — the tone already present in `about_body` and the experience bullets ("Led rigorous code reviews...", "Shrunk app bundle from 85 MB to 32 MB"). No filler adjectives, no hype. Confidence comes from specificity (exact numbers, exact stack names), not from tone.

## Anti-references

Generic AI-SaaS template grammar: uppercase tracked eyebrows above every section, gradient-clip text, the hero-metric-card cliché (big number + small label + gradient accent), identical icon+heading+text card grids, side-stripe borders, glassmorphism as decoration. These read as "AI made this" and undercut the senior-engineer positioning the site exists to prove.

## Design Principles

1. Specificity over adjectives — a number or a named technology beats a claim every time.
2. One idea per screen region — the hero commands attention through hierarchy (name → role → proof → single action), not through repeated decorative scaffolding.
3. Precision in motion — animation exists to reveal information in the right order, not to perform "polish."
4. Bilingual parity — every copy decision must read equally sharp in EN and PT; no idiom that breaks in translation.
5. Restraint as brand — a dark, low-chroma surface (oxblood + steel-teal on near-black) does the "confidence" work; copy and layout should not fight it with extra visual noise.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA baseline. `prefers-reduced-motion` respected across all reveal/stagger animations (already implemented via the global media query). No known user-specific accommodations beyond standard AA contrast and keyboard navigability.
